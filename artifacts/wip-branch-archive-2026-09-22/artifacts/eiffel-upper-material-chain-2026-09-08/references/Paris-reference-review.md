# Paris 1889 photograph review — 2026-09-08

Three actual 1889 photographs were visually inspected in the Paris Musées website using the browser, alongside the institution's visible photographer/date/inventory fields. Inspection was of the displayed catalogue images at normal browser resolution, not downloaded originals or a reconstructed 3D scene. No model, production code or plugin changed; no image or private project file was uploaded anywhere.

## Three usable primary references

1. **Hippolyte Blancard, Champ-de-Mars station rail cars and tracks, Avenue de Suffren,1889; Musée Carnavalet,PH76871.** [Primary catalogue and displayed photograph](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/exposition-universelle-de-1889-wagons-de-la-compagnie-de-l-ouest-rails-de-0). The page classifies it as a photograph and displays CC0. **Actually visible:** multiple parallel tracks across an open foreground; a row of large windowed rail vehicles, including a visibly higher/two-level section; darker end panels and a substantial cropped building at the right. This is a specific transport setting, unlike another generic housing block. **Not established by this view:** rail gauge, vehicle dimensions, train motion/frequency, freight-loading activity, exact full station footprint or the location of every track. It does not document motorcars or a crowded pedestrian street. **Next model use:** a small, accurately placed station-edge scene on the Avenue de Suffren/Champ-de-Mars side, after matching a period plan. Preserve its purposeful open rail apron; do not fill railway space with houses or random pedestrians.

2. **Hippolyte Blancard, basin and dome of the Arts Libéraux pavilion,1889; Musée Carnavalet,PH76814.** [Primary catalogue and displayed photograph](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/exposition-universelle-de-1889-bassin-et-dome-du-pavillon-des-arts-liberaux). The page dates it1889 and identifies it as a photograph, CC0. **Actually visible:** a broad, curved basin rim in the lower foreground; water beside it; a prominent ribbed dome with lantern/finial behind trees; low repeated arches/structures across the middle; a small building and part of an Eiffel leg at the right. The catalogue describes the view as from the tower; the photograph itself should govern the camera match rather than assuming a high aerial viewpoint from that wording. **Not established:** basin depth, true paint colours, hidden elevations, exact roof curvature or water dynamics. Tiny figures are insufficient for a crowd count. **Next model use:** refine the existing pavilion/basin ensemble with its distinctive dome silhouette, surrounding low structures and planted edges. This is a better close/middle-distance focal area than adding more unrelated façade variants.

3. **Hippolyte Blancard, small exposition entrance with attendant,1889; Musée Carnavalet,PH76865.** [Primary catalogue and displayed photograph](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/exposition-universelle-de-1889-petite-entree-de-l-exposition-avec-gardien). Browser-visible metadata confirms1889, photograph and CC0. **Actually visible:** a compact entrance booth with a wide overhanging roof; tall pointed fence boards; an open braced gate; one standing attendant; a mature tree immediately beside the booth; textured ground. These varied human-scale silhouettes are useful against the repeated-building complaint. **Not established:** the precise entrance's position within the entire fair, a queue size, ticketing procedures, colours or booth dimensions. **Next model use:** author one source-matched entrance/booth/fence cluster and place it only after identifying its site. Give pedestrians meaningful destinations and pauses near entrances; the photograph supports an attendant, not an invented dense queue.

## Aerial source for a later city-footprint pass

[Library of Congress item92514593](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/92514593/) identifies Alphonse Liébert's balloon view as an albumen photographic print dated[1889], showing the Seine, Eiffel Tower and exposition buildings. Its known collection image is cph.3b40739; the institutional [Picture This article](https://blogs.loc.gov/picturethis/2015/03/pictures-to-go-up-in-the-air/) also surfaces it. **Metadata was read, but this image was not visually inspected in this pass:** the catalogue/blog browser showed security verification and the web image endpoint failed. No download or challenge workaround was used. Consequently this note makes no new claims about its visible roof parcels, street widths, boats or people. It remains the strongest lead for replacing authored northern-bank blocks with evidence-led block footprints once viewable.

The apparently relevant Carnavalet [“Le Champ de Mars, vue prise du Trocadéro”](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-carnavalet/oeuvres/exposition-universelle-de-1889-le-champ-de-mars-vue-prise-du-trocadero) is catalogued as a **colour photomechanical print/estampe**, not an original photograph. Do not describe it as photographic geometry or use its colour as measured 1889 paint. Previously found Orsay 1888 image metadata was not re-presented as viewed evidence.

## Concrete next modelling boundary

The current city is still explicitly an authored compressed interpretation: `scripts/blender_paris_exposition.py` loads `src/data/eiffelSiteLayout.json`, builds varied parcels and uses a generated façade atlas, while production reads `public/models/paris-1889/paris-city.glb`. The source itself labels the riverfront frontages as unidentified cadastral buildings. More random variations cannot turn those blocks into a documented1889 neighbourhood.

Use photograph2 for one recognisable palace/basin focal reconstruction, photograph3 for a distinct entrance and human activity cluster, and photograph1 for the station edge after plan alignment. Keep the existing river, roads, crowd routes and tower work zone clear until their new footprints are verified. A later accessible aerial photograph plus a period plan should drive joined blocks, roof parcels and street widths on the northern bank. This is a proposal for the next asset revision, not work already carried out.

A single photograph constrains one projection: hidden backs, depth and scale still need other views, plans or explicitly authored assumptions. A photo-aligned first camera is reviewable; a convincing full orbit also requires believable unseen geometry and independent views. None of these sources establishes a complete automatic photo→3D pipeline, and no such reconstruction was run here. The inspected photos also do not justify adding routine motorcar traffic or changing present crowd counts; those need separate dated evidence. Anno1800 can guide the readability of destinations and activity, while these archives govern the historical forms.
