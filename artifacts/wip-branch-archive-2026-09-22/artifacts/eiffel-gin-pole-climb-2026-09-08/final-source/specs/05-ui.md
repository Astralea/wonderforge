# Spec 05 — User Interface

The movie is the product; UI exists to frame it and get out of the way.

## Views & routing

Two views, hash-routed (shareable, back-button friendly):

| Route | View |
|-------|------|
| `#/` | Home: one-page title plate, then catalog, over the same diorama |
| `#/wonder/:id` | Cinematic view for that wonder |

### Authoring route

`#/debug/wonder/:id/:t` renders one chrome-free deterministic frame at normalized
time `t`. Scene authors use the same fixed route before and after edits to compare
silhouette, stage rhythm, foreground work, and reveal lighting without waiting
for a 60-second playback. It is an authoring surface, not gallery navigation.

- `src/store/ui.ts` (`useUiStore`): `{ view, openWonder(id), closeWonder() }`.
- `openWonder` selects the wonder in the playback store, resets it, starts
  playing (unless reduced motion), and writes the hash. `closeWonder` returns
  home and pauses. A `hashchange` listener keeps the store in sync (deep links,
  back/forward).
- Unknown ids in the hash are ignored (fall back to home).

## Home

One viewport. The ambient Giza diorama never leaves the page; Enter does not
scroll. `src/store/ui.ts` holds `homePlate: 'title' | 'catalog'` so returning
from a movie lands on the catalog, not the title. A fresh load is always the
title plate. Reduced motion swaps plates instantly (no CSS transition).

- **Stage**: full-bleed ambient 3D diorama (Pyramids of Giza, parts frozen
  complete, slow orbit, slow day cycle). The ambient orbit is continuous and
  seamless — constant azimuth rate, fixed ensemble target, constant radius,
  breathing pitch — with no periodic snap on loop. A subtle scrim for
  legibility is allowed; no other overlays on the media besides the two
  plates and the soundtrack control.
- **Title plate** (`homePlate === 'title'`): brand **WONDERFORGE** (hero-level,
  display serif), one headline, one short supporting sentence, one CTA
  ("Enter the gallery", pointer cursor on hover). The CTA sets `homePlate` to `catalog`.
- **Catalog plate** (`homePlate === 'catalog'`): the same diorama stays behind
  a left-hand contents veil (full-width on small screens). A compact wordmark
  returns to the title plate. Below it, a vertical index of the 10 wonders —
  NOT cards. Each row: Roman numeral, display-serif name, meta line
  (location · era · year), hairline divider. Hover tints the row with the
  wonder's sky color. Click → `openWonder(id)`. Rows stagger in on first
  reveal. Escape also returns to the title plate. The fan-homage disclaimer
  sits under the index, not on a second scrolled page.

## Cinematic view

- Full-screen `WonderCanvas` in `cinematic` mode; auto-plays on entry.
- **Wonder-owned soundtrack**: soundtrack lookup is keyed by stable wonder ID
  and cue role, never by role alone. A migrated reference scene must ship an
  independently generated, geographically and temporally appropriate cue; it
  may not reuse another wonder's file as a fallback. If a legacy scene has no
  reviewed cue yet, it stays silent. Music briefs distinguish documented
  instrument evidence from cautious speculative scoring and reject later
  cultural shorthand as authenticity (for Stonehenge: no Celtic, Druidic,
  medieval, or bagpipe stereotype; for the Eiffel Tower: 1889 salon strings
  and restrained brass, never accordion, musette, can-can, jazz, or
  Edith Piaf-era chanson). Generated-file provenance, prompt, model,
  duration, and level remain typed data. The Google Gen AI SDK/Lyria pipeline
  is the approved generator for new cues.
- **Title card** (bottom-left) while `t < 0.15`: wonder name, location, era,
  year. Fades out as construction begins. Title and quote sit on the picture,
  not in the auto-hiding chrome, so a picture-first open still names the
  wonder. Their bottom edge stays above the letterbox plus the control rail
  (`6vh + 14rem`) — name, phase label, and gold course never share a row.
- **Quote card** (bottom-left) once `t ≥ 0.15`: the Civ VI narrator quote +
  author, name beneath. Stays through the reveal.
- **Control rail** (bottom-center): one adjacent, labelled control group owns
  every global cinematic action: prev wonder, play/pause, next wonder,
  soundtrack, narration, facts, scrubber (full `t` range),
  phase label (Site preparation / Construction / Complete), replay button when
  `status === 'complete'`, and a 1×/2×/4× speed toggle group (multiplies
  wall-clock advance; `t` stays the single source of truth). There are no
  isolated cinematic controls in the top-right corner. The **WonderForge**
  wordmark returns in the top-left as the single, familiar route back to the
  gallery; there is no duplicate close/X button. The facts panel may retain its
  own close button because it belongs to that panel, not the movie chrome.
  The progress control is the rail's dominant first row: a full-width, visibly
  thick gold course with phase and percentage above it.   The compact action row
  sits beneath, so buttons never squeeze the viewing/scrubbing affordance.
  The **WonderForge** wordmark remains the single route home, parked in
  the top letterbox band; below that band, a **caption beat index** lists
  every authored kicker in movie order (`src/ui/CaptionBeatIndex.tsx`). Idle rows show the title only. Hover or
  keyboard focus reveals that beat's sentence beside a gold hairline; a
  click seeks to the beat's `from` and resumes play (reduced motion still
  lands on the completed still). Past, current, and upcoming rows stay
  visually distinct so finished faces remain reachable without crowding the
  quote card. Documentary upper-left lower-thirds sit below this index.
- **Chrome auto-hide**: the movie opens picture-first. While playing, chrome
  stays away until a pointer move, touch/pen press, or keypress; it fades after ~2.5 s without
  further input. Hovering or focusing the beat index holds chrome open so a
  still cursor does not hide the titles mid-read. Pause and complete keep
  chrome visible. A 6vh cinematic letterbox frames the picture at all
  times. The **WonderForge** wordmark sits in the top bar — vertically
  centered in that 6vh band — so it reads as part of the frame, not as a
  title over the picture. That slot is a fixed viewport position: if the
  bars are not painted, the wordmark does not jump. The beat index sits
  below the top bar with a 1rem gap so the stage titles never rest in the
  black. The wordmark stays in the bar even while the rest of the chrome
  auto-hides; it remains the single route home.
- **Caption layer**: while the movie plays at 1×, short authored captions
  fade in over the picture, hold long enough to be read, and fade out —
  documentary lower-thirds. They remain visible if the chrome is showing;
  they never occupy the quote/title card's lower-left corner, and a
  lower-right beat sits above the control rail so the two text systems
  cannot overlap. Each beat carries a small-caps kicker and
  one sentence of authentic, era-checked content (the same rule as quotes:
  true and attributable to the scene's typed data). Beats are typed data
  (`src/data/captions.ts`): a `t` window, a placement (lower-right /
  upper-left / upper-right), and the text. Beat **count** is authored per
  wonder — two catalog-fact beats, or three to seven documentary faces —
  not a mandatory five-step copy of Giza. The
  envelope is a pure function of playback `t` (`src/engine/captions.ts`),
  so scrubbing and pausing freeze a caption exactly mid-fade. Windows are
  disjoint with gaps between them, and no caption runs into the reveal
  (t ≥ 0.88) — the finale stays clean. Each prerecorded clip must finish
  inside its window with a ≥0.4 s pad so the last word is not cut by the
  next beat. Captions render only at 1× speed
  (at 2×/4× the read time would compress past honesty), never under
  reduced motion (the movie jumps to the completed still), and carry
  `aria-live="polite"` so screen readers hear them in order.   Giza's, Stonehenge's, Petra's, the Colosseum's, Sydney's, and the Eiffel
  Tower's tracks are authored
  against each movie's shot schedule; remaining wonders derive two beats
  from their catalog facts.
- **Caption voice**: an optional, explicitly labelled **Narration on/off**
  control in the bottom control rail reads each caption aloud; it is a playback
  preference, never a microphone/recording control. Caption audio is
  **ElevenLabs only** (`eleven_multilingual_v2`), generated offline by
  `scripts/generate-narration.py` and served as locally bundled MP3s. Typed
  metadata in `src/data/narration.ts` pins each clip to one caption beat and
  records its voice, provenance, duration, and playback level. Each authored
  reference scene ships a **distinct** voice: Giza keeps George; Stonehenge
  uses Daniel (Steady Broadcaster); the Colosseum uses Bill (Wise, Mature,
  Balanced); Sydney Opera House uses Alice (Clear Engaging Educator); the
  Eiffel Tower uses Adam. Never
  reuse another wonder's narrator. On an explicit enable click, the UI
  prepares those local clip elements inside that same user gesture so a later
  caption is permitted to play by browser autoplay policy. **Missing clips,
  failed playback, and wonders without a generated track are silence.** Never
  fall back to the browser's `speechSynthesis`, Vertex Gemini TTS, or any
  other runtime speech API. Never call ElevenLabs from the browser. The
  toggle is off by default and its preference persists across sessions.
  Pausing, seeking, leaving the beat, or toggling off stops the active clip;
  chrome wake no longer hides the caption or cuts the line. If playback
  resumes inside the same beat, voice continues at elapsed movie time rather
  than restarting the sentence. Voice never fires at 2×/4× (captions don't
  render). Narration is level-matched to sit above the soundtrack without
  changing the authoritative playback clock. A typed reference migration is
  incomplete until every authored caption beat has a matching ElevenLabs
  clip.
- **Keyboard**: `Space` play/pause · `←`/`→` prev/next wonder · `R` replay ·
  `Esc` close.

## Typography & color

- Display: **Cinzel** (epigraphic, Civ-flavored). Body: **Alegreya Sans**.
- Theme: "museum at dusk" — deep umber/near-black page (`#14100c` → `#1e1812`
  gradient), parchment text (`#f3ede2`), gold accent (`#d4a24e`).
- Tokens live in `src/index.css` `@theme`; no flat single-color backgrounds.

## Accessibility

- Semantic landmarks (`header`, `main`, `footer`), buttons are real buttons
  with labels; scrubber is a labeled `<input type="range">`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no auto-play; wonder shown complete (`t = 1`) and
  scrubbable; no CSS transitions.
- Focus states visible; canvas is `aria-hidden` (content is conveyed by the
  overlay text).
