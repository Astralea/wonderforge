# Summit mast assemblies and handling

The four 0.6 m crossbars are factory assembled with the mast segment that contains
their final elevation: `summit-crown-m073-c000` with `summit-crown-m072-c000`,
`summit-crown-m074-c000` and `summit-crown-m075-c000` with `summit-crown-m072-c001`,
and `summit-crown-m076-c000` with `summit-crown-m072-c002`. Each original part ID
is retained. Except for the explicitly corrected lower mast joint below, source
meshes and final transforms remain unchanged. The child's pose is the
parent's sampled pose multiplied by its exact final relative transform, from
the receiving stock through transport to seating. There is one lifting job per
assembly. There must be no separate crossbar crane, duplicate seated part, or
late appearance of its crossbar.

Save the actual preassembled source in Blender through the installed MCP and
verify the exported child transforms against the current kit. Historical factory
preassembly supports this approach, but the precise crossbar details and rig are
an authored mechanical reconstruction, not a claim of surveyed 1889 equipment.

The original first mast segment occupied Y298–302.667 while the existing solid
stair column extended to Y300.510. Remove this impossible overlap in Blender:
start the first mast above the existing crown floor hub at approximately
Y300.670, and end it at the
unchanged second mast's underside. Preserve every unrelated kit node, ID and
binary geometry buffer. Shorten the matching completed mast member to the same
lower joint. This is a geometrically plausible butt joint, not a reconstructed
historical connection detail or a structural capacity calculation. The floor
spokes extend above the central stair column, so seating at the column's
Y300.510 top would still penetrate the floor; that candidate is rejected.

Stage the first assembly flat on a carrier supported by the actual west terrace
floor at approximately Y280.590. A fixed 3.4×0.30×2.2 m crane pad at X−3,Z−6.4
fits the north terrace strip; its guy feet use Z±0.95 m. Verify all pad and carrier
corners against actual exported floor triangles, and check the entire revised
route against the campanile and the central stair, including final seating.

Replace disproportionate summit equipment only with fixed geometry whose
bearings attach to actual already-seated structural members. Verify transformed
bearing corners and the combined cargo envelope against the current tower.
Any new work platform must have a complete visible support path. A station
origin near a roof is not proof of support. Preserve earlier floor delivery
chapters and the complete monument geometry.

The current elevated receiving stock still omits part of its supply chain.
Preassembly and route clearance alone do not complete ground-to-summit delivery.
Do not describe the complete construction as mechanically validated until that
chain and equipment installation/removal have been shown or honestly represented.
