# Eiffel construction pace

## Scope

The first physical-time clock covers the live stage-63 summit campaign only.
It does not change the existing 60-second production plan, the film insertion
record, or any historical manifest seal. Stages 0–62 retain their existing
clock until separately reviewed.

Stage 63 contains 79 current kit pieces in 20 dependency-ordered waves of at
most four simultaneous loads. A wave is divided at the production sampler's
existing phase boundaries: staged `0..0.12`, hoist `0.12..0.30`, transfer
`0.30..0.78`, and lower `0.78..1`. The clock preserves those boundaries and is
exactly reversible.

## Physical duration

The final mast wave is the representative foreground operation. Each of its
phases lasts long enough for its slowest load at these review rates:

- 1.5 seconds for rigging on the receiving carrier;
- 0.75 metres per second for vertical hoisting;
- 20 degrees per second for a rigid orientation change, concurrent with the
  vertical hoist;
- 0.65 metres per second along the actual crane transfer arc;
- 0.25 metres per second for the final controlled lowering, with at least
  0.8 seconds for contact and seating.

Every phase also occupies at least one 60 Hz frame. Loads in one wave remain
simultaneous, and later waves cannot overtake earlier dependency waves. Holds
outside the selected stage retain their original duration.

The preceding 19 waves are explicitly cinematic time-lapse. Their duration is
still derived from the sampled path: smoothstep peak motion may advance by no
more than 0.25 metres or rotate by more than 5 degrees in one 60 Hz frame, and
rigging receives four frames. These limits prevent multi-metre single-frame
jumps; they are not claims that a crane physically works at the corresponding
time-lapse speed.

For the 2026-09-08 manifest this maps the stage-63 campaign to
119.69898603056319 seconds and the production clock to 178.88020802139584
seconds. With the already admitted film chapters, the complete Eiffel film is
669.8802080213959 seconds. The long-load chapter still begins at
255.32973290069668 seconds; only the later summit interval changes.

## Honest limit

This clock makes the current elevated handling leg readable. It does not prove
or depict ground provenance. Stage-63 loads still begin on elevated receiving
carriers supplied by an omitted freight chain. The clock must not be described
as a complete ground-to-summit construction sequence, and its admission to the main film carries this explicit unresolved limitation.
The two companion final-wave routes also need current-roof clearance review;
timing and camera tests do not establish that clearance.
