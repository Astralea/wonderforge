## Three visually inspected primary references

All three images were actually opened and visually inspected in Chrome through CUA, rather than accepted from search descriptions. No remote image was downloaded, copied into an asset, used as a texture or republished. Modern photographs guide surviving architectural vocabulary; they do not establish exact 1420 fabric or metric dimensions.

1. **Central Harmony Hall, Palace Museum photograph.** [Official image](https://img.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/Picture/2020/07/03/s5eff05cd7866d.jpg), linked by the museum's [building directory](https://young.dpm.org.cn/info/995). Inspected 1000 × 666 photograph: one continuous four-sided pyramidal roof terminating in a round finial; the eave corners turn upward while most of the eave stays almost level. Deep, dark bracket band above open red columns; a compact square pavilion with substantially more roof overhang than a simple box. White balustrades extend into the surrounding gray paved terrace. **Modeling priority:** one apex, no long ridge, no extra pagoda tier; preserve the open corner columns. Photographer/date/license not established by the image itself; Palace Museum source, reference-only, no redistribution permission assumed.

2. **Preserving Harmony Hall, Palace Museum photograph.** [Official image](https://img.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/Picture/2020/07/03/s5efedbf9edae0.jpg), same [museum directory](https://young.dpm.org.cn/info/995). Inspected 1000 × 666 frontal photograph: long upper ridge with large terminal ornaments, two distinct eave levels, a broad projecting lower roof, shadowed painted brackets and repeated red columns. The center is an open approach, with stone stairs and railings rather than a solid red plinth. **Modeling priority:** the upper roof is narrower than the lower eave footprint and the central ridge is nearly straight; do not bend the entire roof into a bowl. Its **hip-and-gable** classification is corroborated by the museum's [王文涛, 中和殿宝顶](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/pdf/6179/T00120_00.pdf); the frontal image alone does not prove the side gable shape. Photographer/date/license not established; reference-only.

3. **Buildings on the central axis, UNESCO / Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau.** [Record and license](https://whc.unesco.org/en/documents/205926), [actual full image inspected](https://whc.unesco.org/document/205926). Jin Dongjun, 1 July 2019, 5472 × 3648. A compressed oblique/telephoto view shows nested gold roof fields, multiple courtyard walls, white terrace bands and gray subsidiary roofs. Tile surfaces are warm ochre with finely shaded ribs, not metallic yellow sheets. **Modeling priority:** stepped roof hierarchy and intervening courts should remain readable from the orbit; use more restrained materials than the saturated frontal photographs imply. This view is unsuitable for measuring hall spacing because of perspective compression and occlusion. The record specifies attribution / no derivatives; use as a linked visual reference only. Modern trees, city skyline and present furnishings are not evidence for the founding period.

The official Palace Museum `smoelink-img8-tc.jpg` search result was also inspected and **rejected as a three-hall reference**: it shows a palace gate bearing the museum sign, despite the search description calling it the central hall. Do not use that result to justify Supreme Harmony geometry. For Supreme Harmony's double-eaved **hipped** classification, retain the official roof essay above and the rebuilding reference in PRIMARY-REFERENCES.md. Its present form dates to 1695–1697; authored 60 × 36 / 22 × 22 / 44 × 26 metre hall dimensions remain design dimensions, not measurements from these photos.

## Authentic quotation candidate

Original: **「左祖右社，面朝后市。」**

Attribution: **《周礼·考工记》 (Kaogongji / Record of Crafts, in the Zhouli)**. Not Confucius.

New English translation for this project: **“The ancestral temple to the left, the altar of the soil to the right; the court before, the market behind.”**

Verification: Palace Museum, 杨新成, **明代宫室的营建和形制布局简述**, 故宫学刊 2010, printed p. 117 / PDF p. 1, explicitly quotes the passage and cites 《周礼注疏》卷41《冬官考工记下》, 四库全书本. [Official full paper](https://img.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/12/15/u5df5ee91bcc7d.pdf). Full text of this page was read; its page illustration is not claimed as visually inspected. The ancient Chinese passage is public-domain text; the English above is newly translated, not borrowed from a copyrighted modern edition. The modern article itself is not assumed public domain.

This is a city-planning ideal, not a report of the palace's construction methods or a literal inventory of the three-hall scene. If quoted in the film, use the original attribution and a short contextual label such as “An ancient ideal of the imperial capital.” Left/right are ceremonial orientations, not screen-left/right in an arbitrary camera view. Prefer a separate ending quote to forcing this long sentence into an action narration beat.
