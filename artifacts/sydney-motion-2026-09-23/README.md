## Fixes and evidence

**Scenery flicker:** Sydney's 0.5 m near plane combined with a 12 km far plane lost the depth separation of distant windows and road surfaces. The scene now uses a 20 m near plane. At 1.5 km, a 25 mm separation formerly occupied less than one 24-bit depth level; it now occupies more than three. The actual environment bounds stay outside the near frustum through the complete desktop/portrait camera path. `scripts/probe-sydney-background.mjs` freezes construction and lighting and rotates only the camera, rendering the identical view at near=0.5, 20 and 100 m. Across 31 wide-view angles, pixels differing by more than 12 RGB levels from the 100 m reference fall from 47,751 to 262 (99.45%). See `final-depth-rotation/report.json` and its matched frames. This isolates the depth artifact; it is not a claim that every shader or edge has zero aliasing on every GPU.

**Workers:** the old scaffold station IDs [0,3,6] were all absent from the current shell-safe falsework. Workers now use the actual erected bays, with footprints inside the decks and feet on their top surfaces. Unsupported climbing poses are removed because the scene has no corresponding ladders. Twelve persistent yard workers walk/work at film-second rates. Trolley crews face the route instead of the geometry's baked rotation, and stay beside the transported part. Ground signalmen wave; they are no longer described as holding unrendered tag lines. Worker height is approximately 1.88 m. A smooth yard insert during the Opera rib's preparation/haul makes people readable without enlarging them; its four-second pullback keeps the entire lifted hero bounds within both viewports. Exact screen-space visibility, support, motion, frame displacement and reverse-sampling contracts are in `tests/sydney-motion-readability.test.ts`.

**Boats:** five continuous non-wrapping voyages and three berths reuse all eight original boats, with unchanged hull/cabin/mast draw calls. Heading follows each curve, every piece follows one rigid pose, keels intersect the water and cabins/masts sit on deck. Actual transformed geometry is sampled over 301 times for a 1 m shoreline buffer, wharf/bridge clearance, more than 3 m vessel separation, contact and repeatability. Routes travel about 63–145 m over the film; projected motion was 1.9–4.3 CSS px/s desktop and 1.0–2.6 portrait around .58.

**Cranes:** one pure per-crane lifecycle now drives the renderer during loaded work and empty-hook return. Persistent cables and hooks replace the fixed idle fallback. Previously, actual rendered jib tips jumped 25–41 m at attachment and 111–145 m at release. Boundary samples now agree within 1 mm; jib length remains fixed. Hero preparation and return receive reserved time, while existing 4.2 s rib and 3 s tile operation durations remain. Tests check actual renderer matrices, loaded hook correspondence, single-crane ownership, phase visibility and forward/reverse seeks. Hero evidence is regenerated from the current plan, not copied old timestamps.

## Physical and historical limits

The roof uses the retained exact spherical geometry and dependency/support contracts. This pass checks the rendered machinery, load hook, rigid dimensions, crew support, boat contact and route clearances; it is not a structural engineering certification or an exact historical reconstruction.

The real roof relied on travelling steel erection arches, post-tensioning and lateral connections; this film still represents erection support with static scaffold towers and does not animate prestressing. Arup's primary construction account describes that difference: [Arup Journal, September 1973](https://www.arup.com/globalassets/downloads/arup-journal/the-arup-journal-1973-issue-3.pdf). The official Opera House/Arup account also describes site construction technologies: [Delivering new technologies](https://stream.sydneyoperahouse.com/videos/tristram-carfrae-rdi-delivering-new-technologies). Static crane bases and fewer, larger panels remain disclosed cinematic compression.

The 60-second film compresses hundreds of ordinary assembly operations. Its background cranes can still cross roughly 175 m in a sampled frame; hero motion is slower but also compressed. The lifecycle discontinuities are fixed, but this is not a simulation of real crane velocities. A future full choreography redesign would need fewer simultaneously narrated operations or a longer film, and a travelling support-arch sequence. Broad city-art sparsity also remains outside this repair.
