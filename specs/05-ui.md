# Spec 05 — User Interface

The movie is the product; UI exists to frame it and get out of the way.

## Views & routing

Two views, hash-routed (shareable, back-button friendly):

| Route | View |
|-------|------|
| `#/` | Home: one-page title plate, then catalog, over the same diorama |
| `#/wonder/:id` | Cinematic view for that wonder |

### Authoring route

`#/debug/wonder/:id/:t` renders one chrome-free deterministic frame at normalized
time `t`. Scene authors use the same fixed route before and after edits to compare
silhouette, stage rhythm, foreground work, and reveal lighting without waiting
for a 60-second playback. It is an authoring surface, not gallery navigation.

- `src/store/ui.ts` (`useUiStore`): `{ view, openWonder(id), closeWonder() }`.
- `openWonder` selects the wonder in the playback store, resets it, starts
  playing (unless reduced motion), and writes the hash. `closeWonder` returns
  home and pauses. A `hashchange` listener keeps the store in sync (deep links,
  back/forward).
- Unknown ids in the hash are ignored (fall back to home). Unpublished
  catalog ids (`petra`, `chichen-itza`, `machu-picchu`, `angkor-wat`,
  `forbidden-city`, `sydney-opera-house`) are also ignored: they remain in
  typed data and authoring routes, but they are not gallery movies.

## Home

One viewport. The ambient diorama never leaves the page; Enter does not
scroll. `src/store/ui.ts` holds `homePlate: 'title' | 'catalog'` so returning
from a movie lands on the catalog, not the title. A fresh load is always the
title plate. Reduced motion swaps plates instantly (no CSS transition).

- **Stage**: full-bleed ambient 3D diorama of the **Pyramids of Giza**,
  parts frozen complete, slow orbit, slow day cycle. Giza is the homepage
  default; do not pick Colosseum (or any other On site film) for this
  orbit. Ignore a stored `wf-home-wonder` that is not Giza. The ambient
  orbit is continuous and
  seamless — constant azimuth rate, fixed ensemble target, constant radius,
  breathing pitch — with no periodic snap on loop. A subtle scrim for
  legibility is allowed; no other overlays on the media besides the two
  plates and the soundtrack control. Homepage preparation shows no loading
  animation, percentage, or minimum presentation delay; the title is available
  immediately while the ambient scene prepares behind it.
- **Title plate** (`homePlate === 'title'`): brand **WonderForge** is the
  semantic page heading. The visible mark is the illustrated **WONDER /
  FORGE** lockup (`/brand/wonderforge-wordmark.png`): each letter is a
  monument, not display-serif type. The PNG is a flood-fill knockout of
  the painted dark field (true alpha from the edges, letter interiors
  kept). Dust, sparkle-rays, and leftover field above the W (Great Wall)
  are punched with that field — the letter is the wall, not the glow
  cloud. Show the full frame — do not crop, zoom, or mix-blend the plate.
  No promotional slogan and no
  explanatory sentence — do not describe the medium, the films, or the
  construction from the title plate. The picture is the site; narration
  belongs in the movie. One CTA (“Choose a site”). The CTA sets `homePlate`
  to `catalog`. Do not name the medium on the button: the visitor is already
  standing in a yard, and the next plate is an index of other sites.
- **Catalog plate** (`homePlate === 'catalog'`): the same diorama stays behind
  a left-hand contents veil (full-width on small screens). Typeset
  **WonderForge** (Cinzel, same chrome as before the lockup) returns to the
  title plate (`aria-label`: “WonderForge, back to introduction”). The
  illustrated lockup stays on the title plate only.
  The index is **two blocks**, not “ten wonders”: **On site** then
  **In production**. Do not use “Watch now” or “Experience now” — those name
  a medium or a product pitch; the visitor is choosing a yard. Each block is
  chronological by `completedYear` (oldest first). On-site rows are the same
  index treatment as before (Roman numeral, name, meta, hover tint) plus a
  trailing wonder-owned glyph. Each glyph is a distinctive architectural
  line drawing (Stonehenge is a sarsen horseshoe of trilithons, not one
  lintel; the Colosseum is a four-storey arcade with the ruined wing, not
  a single oval). The glyph slot is empty until hover or
  keyboard focus; then the gold line drawing builds from the ground up
  as if one pen were drawing it: strokes are typed data (`d`, build
  stage, weight) and a pure planner (`src/ui/wonderGlyphTiming.ts`)
  measures each path so duration is proportional to its length (constant
  pen speed, clamped so ticks never pop) and stages start in order with a
  slight overlap — ground line, then footings, then walls, then crowns.
  Symmetric forms (pyramid flanks, Eiffel legs, Angkor spires) are split so
  both sides rise to meet at the apex rather than one side descending.
  A faint dotted plan of the finished drawing appears at once under the
  ink so hover feedback is immediate; fresh ink is a hotter, paler gold and
  cools to the row color once its stroke lands; the whole drawing rises a
  few percent from a ground-anchored origin as it builds (the ground line
  itself does not move). Leaving the row fades
  the drawing out (never rewinds it) and resets it for the next hover.
  Reduced motion shows the completed drawing instantly on hover with no
  transitions. Click → `openWonder(id)`.
  In-production rows are visible but not a control: dashed outline, no fade,
  no pointer cursor, no `openWonder`. They use the same empty-then-build
  glyph on hover, but the row is not a control. Do not add a second sentence
  under In production.
  Prev/next in a movie walk only the
  On site list. Rows stagger in on first reveal. Escape also returns to the
  title plate. The catalog has no legal footer, fan-homage line, affiliation
  disclaimer, or asset-origin note.
- **Pointer cursor**: every clickable chrome control uses the pointer (hand)
  cursor — title CTA, catalog wordmark, catalog rows, soundtrack and
  narration toggles, cinematic wordmark, beat-index rows and the Chapters
  toggle, transport and speed buttons, the seek course, facts open/close,
  and scene-reload. The diorama canvas is the picture, not a control, and
  keeps the default cursor.

## Cinematic view

- Full-screen `WonderCanvas` in `cinematic` mode; auto-plays on entry.
- **Wonder-owned soundtrack**: soundtrack lookup is keyed by stable wonder ID
  and cue role, never by role alone. A migrated reference scene must ship an
  independently generated, geographically and temporally appropriate cue; it
  may not reuse another wonder's file as a fallback. If a legacy scene has no
  reviewed cue yet, it stays silent. Music briefs distinguish documented
  instrument evidence from cautious speculative scoring and reject later
  cultural shorthand as authenticity (for Stonehenge: no Celtic, Druidic,
  medieval, or bagpipe stereotype; for the Eiffel Tower: 1889 salon strings
  and restrained brass, never accordion, musette, can-can, jazz, or
  Edith Piaf-era chanson). Generated-file provenance, prompt, model,
  duration, and level remain typed data. The Google Gen AI SDK/Lyria pipeline
  is the approved generator for new cues. Opening a Ready film from the
  catalog (or prev/next) primes that wonder's cinematic cue inside the same
  click so autoplay policy does not silence it. Priming is an unlock: the
  element plays and immediately pauses, and the score stays silent over the
  loader. The first audible start is the first ready frame. The hook reuses
  that element and does not pause a film that has already started if the canvas
  later marks assets unready. Ready-frame start places the cue on the film
  clock once; it must not restart a bed that already began with the picture.
  Direct URLs may still need a later Play gesture; Play/Replay in the rail
  primes the same way.
- **Title card** (bottom-left) until the earlier of `t = 0.15` or the first authored chapter: wonder name, location, era,
  year. Fades out as construction begins. Title and quote sit on the picture,
  not in the auto-hiding chrome, so a picture-first open still names the
  wonder. Their bottom edge stays above the letterbox plus the control rail
  (`6vh + 14rem`) — name, phase label, and gold course never share a row.
- **Quote card**: construction keeps the picture free of the quotation;
  non-Eiffel films show it on desktop after both `t ≥ 0.9` and their last
  authored chapter have ended. Eiffel
  keeps its authored closing window. The title leaves when the first chapter begins, or at `t = 0.15`.
  Portrait shows the name in the upper safe area, with the full attributed
  quote available in **About this wonder** throughout the film. Never stack
  a long quotation over an active operation or a chapter sentence.
- **Control rail** (bottom-center): one adjacent, labelled control group owns
  every global cinematic action: prev wonder, play/pause, next wonder,
  soundtrack, narration, facts, scrubber (full `t` range),
  phase label (Site preparation / Construction / Complete), replay button when
  `status === 'complete'`, and a 1×/2×/4× speed toggle group (multiplies
  wall-clock advance; `t` stays the single source of truth). There are no
  isolated cinematic controls in the top-right corner. Typeset
  **WonderForge** (Cinzel) returns in the top-left as the single, familiar
  route back to the gallery; there is no duplicate close/X button. The
  illustrated lockup does not appear in cinematic chrome. The facts panel may retain its
  own close button because it belongs to that panel, not the movie chrome.
  The progress control is the rail's dominant first row: a full-width, visibly
  thick gold course with phase and percentage above it. The scrubber's
  accessible name is **Film position**. Replay (when complete) is **Replay film**.
  Facts open as **About this wonder** and close as **Close information panel**.
  Speed buttons stay visibly `1×` / `2×` / `4×` with accessible names
  **Playback speed: {value} times**. The compact action row
  sits beneath, so buttons never squeeze the viewing/scrubbing affordance.
  The **WonderForge** wordmark remains the single route home
  (`aria-label`: “WonderForge, back to films”), parked in
  the top letterbox band; below that band, a **film chapter index** lists
  every authored kicker in movie order (`src/ui/CaptionBeatIndex.tsx`). The
  accessible group name is “Film chapters”. Idle rows show the title only. Hover or
  keyboard focus reveals that beat's sentence beside a gold hairline; a
  click seeks to the beat's `from` and resumes play (`Play from {title}`),
  while reduced motion still lands on the completed still (`Show {title}`).
  At narrow widths every film uses a **Chapters** disclosure in the top
  letterbox; an expanded index temporarily replaces the caption, then closes
  when a chapter is selected. Desktop retains the full index in a bounded, scrollable region; the active
  row stays in view for any authored chapter count.
  Past, current, and upcoming rows stay
  visually distinct so finished faces remain reachable without crowding the
  quote card. Documentary upper-left lower-thirds sit below this index.
- **Chrome auto-hide**: the movie opens picture-first. While playing, chrome
  stays away until a pointer move, touch/pen press, or keypress; it fades after ~2.5 s without
  further input. Hovering or focusing the beat index holds chrome open so a
  still cursor does not hide the titles mid-read. Pause and complete keep
  chrome visible. A 6vh cinematic letterbox frames the picture at all
  times. Typeset **WonderForge** sits in the top bar — vertically
  centered in that 6vh band — so it reads as part of the frame, not as a
  title over the picture. That slot is a fixed viewport position: if the
  bars are not painted, the wordmark does not jump. The beat index sits
  below the top bar with a 1rem gap so the stage titles never rest in the
  black. The typeset wordmark stays in the bar even while the rest of the chrome
  auto-hides; it remains the single route home.
- **Caption layer**: while the movie plays at 1×, short authored captions
  fade in over the picture, hold long enough to be read, and fade out —
  documentary lower-thirds. They remain visible if the chrome is showing;
  they never occupy the quote/title card's lower-left corner, and a
  lower-right beat sits above the control rail so the two text systems
  cannot overlap. Each beat carries a small-caps kicker and
  one sentence of authentic, era-checked content (the same rule as quotes:
  true and attributable to the scene's typed data). Beats are typed data
  (`src/data/captions.ts`): a `t` window, a placement (lower-right /
  upper-left / upper-right), and the text. Beat **count** is authored per
  wonder, with no minimum, maximum, fixed quota, or inherited schedule.
  Distinct historical events and visible construction determine the count. The
  envelope is a pure function of playback `t` (`src/engine/captions.ts`),
  so scrubbing and pausing freeze a caption exactly mid-fade. Windows are
  disjoint with breathing space between them. Each film authors its own
  opening and closing windows: an evening or alignment chapter may accompany
  the reveal, followed by an unspoken final hold. Each prerecorded clip must finish
  inside its window with a ≥0.4 s pad so the last word is not cut by the
  next beat. Captions render only at 1× speed
  (at 2×/4× the read time would compress past honesty), never under
  reduced motion (the movie jumps to the completed still), and carry
  `aria-live="polite"` so screen readers hear them in order.   Giza's, Stonehenge's, Petra's, the Colosseum's, Sydney's, and the Eiffel
  Tower's tracks are authored
  against each movie's shot schedule; remaining wonders derive two beats
  from their catalog facts.
- **Caption voice**: an optional, explicitly labelled **Narration on/off**
  control in the bottom control rail reads each caption aloud; accessible
  names are **Turn narration on** / **Turn narration off**. It is a playback
  preference, never a microphone/recording control. Caption audio is
  **ElevenLabs only** (`eleven_multilingual_v2`), generated offline by
  `scripts/generate-narration.py` and served as locally bundled MP3s. Typed
  metadata in `src/data/narration.ts` pins each clip to one caption beat and
  records its voice, provenance, duration, and playback level. Each authored
  reference scene ships a **distinct** voice: Giza uses Charles (Expressive,
  Mature and Clear); Stonehenge uses Oliver (Clean, British and Steady);
  the Colosseum uses Andrea Williams; Sydney Opera House uses Alice (Clear Engaging Educator); the
  Eiffel Tower uses Adam. Never
  reuse another wonder's narrator. On an explicit enable click, the UI
  prepares those local clip elements inside that same user gesture so a later
  caption is permitted to play by browser autoplay policy. **Missing clips,
  failed playback, and wonders without a generated track are silence.** Never
  fall back to the browser's `speechSynthesis`, Vertex Gemini TTS, or any
  other runtime speech API. Never call ElevenLabs from the browser. The
  toggle is off by default except for Eiffel's first-use story narration in
  Spec 34; an explicit preference persists across sessions and always wins.
  Pausing, seeking, leaving the beat, or toggling off stops the active clip;
  chrome wake no longer hides the caption or cuts the line. If playback
  resumes inside the same beat, voice continues at elapsed movie time rather
  than restarting the sentence. Voice never fires at 2×/4× (captions don't
  render). Narration is level-matched to sit above the soundtrack without
  changing the authoritative playback clock. A typed reference migration is
  incomplete until every authored caption beat has a matching ElevenLabs
  clip.
- **Eiffel chapter captions** (Spec 34): construction transitions keep the world
  visible and playback continuous. Compact floating story captions replace the
  old full-screen cards; their local Adam clips use elapsed film time while the
  production clock is held. They share the existing 1×/pause/narration policy.
- **Keyboard**: `Space` play/pause · `←`/`→` prev/next wonder · `R` replay ·
  `Esc` close.

## Typography & color

- Display: **Cinzel** (epigraphic, Civ-flavored). Body: **Alegreya Sans**.
- Theme: "museum at dusk" — deep umber/near-black page (`#14100c` → `#1e1812`
  gradient), parchment text (`#f3ede2`), gold accent (`#d4a24e`).
- Tokens live in `src/index.css` `@theme`; no flat single-color backgrounds.

## Accessibility

- Semantic landmarks (`header`, `main`, `footer`), buttons are real buttons
  with labels; scrubber is a labeled `<input type="range">`.
- Clickable chrome uses `cursor: pointer` (the hand). Disabled chrome uses
  `not-allowed`. Author this in the shared UI stylesheet so a new button
  inherits it; do not leave interactive controls on the default arrow.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no auto-play; wonder shown complete (`t = 1`) and
  scrubbable; no CSS transitions.
- Focus states visible; canvas is `aria-hidden` (content is conveyed by the
  overlay text).
